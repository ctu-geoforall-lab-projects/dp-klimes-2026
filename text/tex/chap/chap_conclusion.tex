This thesis investigated cross-sensor transfer learning for cloud semantic segmentation on satellite imagery, focusing on the transfer of knowledge from Sentinel-2 to VEN$\mu$S. All goals of the thesis were successfully achieved. The theoretical foundations of cross-sensor transfer learning for cloud detection were established, source and target datasets were prepared and preprocessed, a deep learning model was trained and evaluated under multiple transfer learning and normalization strategies. The results were compared against a from-scratch deep learning baseline and the MAJA classical cloud detection algorithm.

The results confirm that effective cross-sensor transfer is achievable across all investigated settings. In the transductive setting, spectral band alignment alone proved insufficient, but dynamic Z-score normalization enabled the pretrained model to generalize to VEN$\mu$S without any modification to the model weights. A notable finding was that shadow detection capability was successfully transferred to VEN$\mu$S in this setting. In the inductive setting, frozen encoder fine-tuning provided the optimal degree of network adaptation, outperforming both output layer fine-tuning and full fine-tuning. All fine-tuning strategies achieved performance comparable to the from-scratch baseline, with frozen encoder fine-tuning surpassing it.

As part of this work, a modular preprocessing pipeline and evaluation and visualization scripts were developed and are publicly available in the thesis repository,\footnote{\url{https://github.com/ctu-geoforall-lab-projects/dp-klimes-2026}} and contributions were made to the open-source cnn-lib library.\footnote{\url{https://github.com/ctu-geoforall-lab/cnn-lib}}

The main limitations of the study are discussed in Section~\ref{sec:discussion_limitations}. Future work could investigate spectral adaptation approaches that remove the requirement for spectrally equivalent bands, more recent transformer-based architectures, and evaluation on additional sensor pairs or more geographically diverse domains. Overall, the results confirm that when the source and target domains are sufficiently similar, transfer learning can match or even exceed the performance of a model trained entirely on target domain data.
